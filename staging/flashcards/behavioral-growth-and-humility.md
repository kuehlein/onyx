# Growth & Humility

Seeking feedback, owning mistakes without minimizing, and visibly getting better.
Interviewers (especially Google — intellectual humility) look for a specific piece
of hard feedback, what you actually changed, and a non-defensive stance (Amazon:
Learn and Be Curious).

## Prompts
- Tell me about the most useful critical feedback you've received. What did you do with it?
- Describe a time you were wrong. How did you realize it and respond?
- Tell me about a skill you deliberately taught yourself to solve a problem.
- Describe a time you changed how you work based on a setback.
- How do you seek out feedback rather than wait for it?
- Tell me about a strong opinion you updated when the evidence changed.

## What strong looks like
A specific, genuinely uncomfortable piece of feedback (not a humblebrag), a concrete
behavior change, and evidence it stuck — plus non-defensiveness and curiosity.
Owns the mistake plainly without minimizing or blaming. Backend flavor: a design
you'd revisit, a habit change after an outage, self-teaching a system to unblock a
project. **Senior/Staff**: also seeks feedback proactively and models it for others.
Weak/penalized: "my weakness is I work too hard", defensiveness, blaming
circumstances, a "lesson" with no actual change, or an ancient trivial example.
