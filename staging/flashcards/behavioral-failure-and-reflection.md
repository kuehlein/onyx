# Failure & Reflection

A real failure, owned honestly, with a specific and non-obvious lesson that changed
what you did next. This is where authenticity is tested hardest — the reflection
("what I'd do differently") is the highest-signal part and the one most candidates
skip (the "+L" that Google's rubric hinges on).

## Prompts
- Tell me about a significant failure. What happened and what did you do?
- Describe a project that didn't go as planned. What was your part in it?
- Tell me about a time you missed a deadline or shipped a bug that mattered.
- Describe a decision you regret. What would you do differently now?
- Tell me about an outage or incident you were responsible for.
- What's a risk you took that didn't pay off?

## What strong looks like
A genuine failure with real stakes where the candidate owns *their* contribution
(not "the project failed" in passive voice), a clear-eyed account of what went
wrong, and a **specific, non-obvious learning** they then applied — ideally with
evidence the change stuck. Calm, non-defensive, no scapegoating. Backend flavor: an
outage, a bad migration, an under-tested launch — with the concrete process/design
change afterward (a guardrail, a rollback plan, a test). **Senior/Staff**: the
learning generalized (a mechanism or standard), not just "I'll be more careful".
Weak/penalized: a fake failure ("I cared too much"), blaming others/circumstances,
no personal ownership, or a lesson that's generic with no change made.
