# Conflict & Backbone

Respectful disagreement on something that mattered — then commitment to the
outcome either way. This is the most common gap and, at Amazon, a missing genuine
disagreement story is close to a dealbreaker (LPs: Have Backbone; Disagree and
Commit, Earn Trust). Interviewers want a real stake, your actual position, how it
resolved, and that you disagreed *and then committed*.

## Prompts
- Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager or a senior engineer. What did you do?
- Describe a technical decision you pushed back on. How did it end?
- Tell me about a time you were overruled. How did you handle it afterward?
- Describe a disagreement with a peer team that you had to resolve.
- Tell me about a time you had to defend an unpopular position with data.
- When did you change your mind after disagreeing? What convinced you?

## What strong looks like
A genuine, non-trivial disagreement with real stakes; a clear personal position
backed by data or reasoning; a concrete resolution; and — critically —
**disagree-then-commit**: once the call was made, you got behind it fully. Candor
without being combative; you preserved the relationship (Earn Trust). Backend
flavor: an architecture/tradeoff/tech-debt or reliability call. **Senior** =
disagreement with peers/manager, resolved constructively. **Staff** = the conflict
is *organizational* (competing teams/leaders) and you influenced **upward** —
disagreeing only laterally or downward under-levels the story. Weak/penalized: no
real conflict, blaming the other side, "I was right and they finally saw it" with
no commitment, or a trivial code-review nit dressed up as backbone.
