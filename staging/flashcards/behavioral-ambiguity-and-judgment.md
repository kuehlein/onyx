# Ambiguity & Judgment

Making a good call and moving forward on incomplete information — then being right
often enough. Interviewers look for how you cut through a vague problem: what you
did to reduce uncertainty, the judgment call you made, and why (Amazon: Are Right
A Lot, Dive Deep; Google: comfort with ambiguity + bias for action).

## Prompts
- Tell me about the most ambiguous problem you've owned. How did you approach it?
- Describe a decision you made without all the data you wanted.
- Tell me about a time you had to define the problem before you could solve it.
- Describe a time you dug into the details to find the real root cause.
- How did you decide what NOT to do on an under-specified project?
- Tell me about a call you made that turned out wrong — how did you course-correct?

## What strong looks like
A concrete method for reducing ambiguity (talked to users/stakeholders, prototyped,
instrumented, framed options), a decisive judgment call with the reasoning, and
data/detail to back it (Dive Deep — comfortable going one level below the summary).
Shows calibrated confidence, not paralysis or recklessness. Backend flavor: an
undefined migration, a murky incident root-cause, a greenfield design with unknown
scale. **Senior** = drove clarity within a bounded scope. **Staff** = set direction
under org-level ambiguity and made bets others followed. Weak/penalized: waited for
someone else to define it, analysis paralysis, a "gut call" with no method or
follow-up, can't go below the headline when probed.
