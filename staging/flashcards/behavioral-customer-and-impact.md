# Customer & Impact Orientation

Starting from the customer/business problem and insisting on outcomes that matter,
not just shipping. Interviewers look for evidence you worked backward from a real
user or business need and refused to accept a low-quality result (Amazon: Customer
Obsession, Insist on Highest Standards; Meta: long-term impact).

## Prompts
- Tell me about a time you dug into what customers/users actually needed.
- Describe a time you pushed back on a requirement because it wasn't right for users.
- Tell me about raising the quality bar even when it was easier to ship as-is.
- Describe a decision where you chose long-term impact over a quick win.
- Tell me about acting on a customer pain point others had overlooked.
- How did you balance velocity against reliability/quality for users?

## What strong looks like
Worked backward from a concrete user/business problem, used evidence (metrics,
support tickets, user signals), and connected the work to real impact. Held a high
bar — built a mechanism to keep quality up, not a one-time heroics. Backend flavor:
reliability/latency/correctness as a *user* outcome, an SLO defended, a data issue
fixed at the source. **Senior** = owned a feature/system's user outcome. **Staff** =
long-term/strategic impact across the org. Weak/penalized: feature-factory "I built
what I was told", no user/business connection, quality framed only as personal
preference, or impact asserted with no evidence.
